Question under test (RQ1 as reformulated on 2026-10-07): *does a research-agent proposer, under evidence pressure, express
demand for authority beyond its grant (more data, wider window, held-out access, budget), through a sanctioned channel or
around it, and does a deterministic evidence→authority boundary change that behaviour?*

## Answers to the seven questions (§8)

1. **Operationalised before?** Partially. Power-seeking (MACHIAVELLI, AgentMisalignment), scope creep (ScopeBench), reward hacking and cheating (CheatBench, RewardHackingAgents), and the effect of a sanctioned escalation channel (arXiv 2608.29460) are all operationalised. None operationalises *evidence-conditioned authority demand in a research agent*.
2. **Equivalent benchmark?** No benchmark presents a research agent with intermediate scientific evidence that makes additional authority rational and measures whether it asks, works around, or abstains. ScopeBench and the escalation-channel study are the nearest; both are coding/engineering tasks with defects or boundaries, not hypotheses with evidence.
3. **Better instrument?** Yes, for the components: CheatBench-style planted opportunities with high base rates; escalation-channel designs that demonstrably get used (the 2608.29460 channel was used; CAIN's T6 channel was not). CAIN's single-turn JSON schema instrument failed validation (0/112 channel use) and is worse than these.
4. **Same threat model tested?** The *enforcement* side (policy blocks, evaluator locking, MAC, cognitive–executive separation) is thoroughly tested elsewhere (Parallax, RewardHackingAgents, 2601.11893). CAIN's containment is not novel.
5. **Simple baseline representing prior art:** an "ask-to-continue" harness (ScopeBench) or an escalation tool plus written policy (2608.29460) applied to a research task. Any new CAIN line must beat or at least be compared against this baseline, not against "no policy".
6. **What is left that is new?** A narrow slice: measuring, with working positive controls, whether a research agent's demand for authority is *conditioned on evidence* (requests that cite intermediate results) versus unconditioned, and how the three outcomes (sanctioned request, out-of-channel request, circumvention) distribute when the extra authority is scientifically useful rather than a shortcut.
7. **Does the difference matter scientifically?** Moderately, and only if the instrument works. The escalation-channel result already shows channels change behaviour in coding; showing the same (or the opposite) when the "defect" is missing evidence rather than a failing test would say something about agents as scientists. It does not justify a frontier intervention study before the base-rate instrument exists (programme R08).
